Things that go UP

Things that go DOWN

Going down stairs 🪜, spending money 💸, the temperature falling on a snowy night ❄️, a diver sinking into the sea 🤿.

🤔 Quick think — can you guess?

Rina climbs UP 3 steps. We can call that +3. Now she goes DOWN 2 steps. What number could show down 2?

Material 1 • Definition

What Are Integers? 📖

⭐ Definition

Integers are whole numbers that can be positive, negative, or zero. Integers have no fractions and no decimals.

🔤 Mathematicians write the whole integer family with the symbol ℤ. The word integer comes from Latin and means whole or untouched — a number that is complete, with nothing broken off like a fraction!

✅ These ARE integers

… −4−3−2−1 0 1234 …

❌ These are NOT integers

½ 0.5 2¾ −1.2 3.14 0.001

Fractions and decimals are useful numbers too — they just do not belong to the integer family. 🧩

Material 2 • Members of the integers

The Integer Family 👨‍👩‍👧‍👦

The integer family has exactly three groups. Meet them all!

🌡️

Negative integers

…, −3, −2, −1

Always smaller than zero. They wear a minus sign (−): cold temperatures, debts, basements, depths.

⚖️

Zero

0

The middle hero! Zero is an integer, but it is neither negative nor positive.

🚀

Positive integers

1, 2, 3, 4, …

Always greater than zero. The + sign is optional: +7 and 7 mean the same thing.

❌ No fractions like ½ ❌ No decimals like 2.4 ✅ Whole steps only!

Material 3 • The number line

The Interactive Number Line 🐸

The number line is the map of the integer world. Our frog friend will help you explore it!

➡️

Right = bigger

Numbers get bigger as you move to the right: −2 < 0 < 3.

⬅️

Left = smaller

Numbers get smaller as you move to the left: 4 > 1 > −7.

🪞

Mirror twins

3 and −3 are the same distance from 0, on opposite sides. Zero sits exactly in the middle.

Material 4 • Around us

Integers in Daily Life 🌍

Integer hunters, look around! Here are the favourite hiding places of positive and negative numbers.

🌡️

Temperature

−5°C means 5 degrees below zero — freezing! +30°C is a warm afternoon.

🏢

Elevators

Basement 2 (B2) is written as floor −2. The ground floor is 0, and floor 5 is up high.

💰

Money

Saving Rp5,000 is +5,000, but owing Rp5,000 to a friend is −5,000 — less than zero!

🌊

Sea level

Sea level is 0. A fish swims at −120 m while a plane flies at +9,000 m.

🎮

Games & sports

Win 250 points → +250. Lose 3 lives → −3. In golf, finishing 2 under par is a brilliant −2!

🕐

Time zones

Jakarta is UTC+7 (7 hours ahead). New York is UTC−5 (5 hours behind).

Material 5 • Comparing

Comparing Integers ⚖️

⭐ The golden rule

The number further to the right on the number line is always the bigger number.

📏 Order of power

Positives beat zero, and zero beats negatives:  5 > 0 > −5. Every negative is smaller than every positive.

⚠️ Careful with negatives!

−8 is smaller than −2, because −8 sits further left. Think of debts: owing 8 feels worse than owing 2!

🐊 The hungry crocodile

The open mouth of < and > always eats the bigger number: 3 > −4, and −1 < 6. Equal numbers use =.

Material 6 • Ordering

Ordering Integers 📊

🪜 3 easy steps

1. Picture the number line.
2. Place every number on it.
3. Read the numbers in order. Left → right gives ascending; right → left gives descending.

📚 Two important words

Ascending ↑ (smallest → largest), like climbing stairs.
Descending ↓ (largest → smallest), like sliding down.

✏️ Worked example — order ascending: −3, +4, 0, −1, +2

Place them on the line: −3 and −1 on the left of 0; then 0; then +2 and +4. Reading left to right gives:

Wrap-up

Conclusion 🌟

Today you became an Integer Explorer! Let's remember the big ideas of our adventure:

  1. Integers are whole numbers: negative, zero, and positive. Fractions and decimals are not part of the family.
  2. On the number line, numbers grow bigger to the right and smaller to the left — zero sits in the middle.
  3. Things below a level — below zero, below sea level, underground, or in debt — are written with a minus sign (−).
  4. To compare, use <, > or =. Remember: −8 < −2 because −8 is further left on the line.
  5. To order, place all numbers on the line, then read left→right for ascending or right→left for descending.
  6. Integers are everywhere: temperatures, elevators, money, sea level, games and time zones. Practice makes perfect — you solved 30 story problems today!
